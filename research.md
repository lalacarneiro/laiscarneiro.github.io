      <p class="strand__figure">1<span>Naming the impact</span></p>
      <div>
        <h3>A typology of ecological impacts</h3>
        <p>
          Existing frameworks either miss whole categories of impact or confuse the
          mechanism — predation, competition, disease — with the consequence. I worked
          on a typology that separates the two and covers the full range of ecological
          effects, so that two studies of two different species can be placed on the
          same map.
        </p>
        <p>
          Related: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2025.03.010" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Typology of the ecological impacts of biological invasions</a>
          and <a href="https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3003665" target="_blank" rel="noopener">expanding impact assessment to the ecosystem level</a>.
        </p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <div class="strand">
      <p class="strand__figure">2<span>Scoring the severity</span></p>
      <div>
        <h3>From categories to a comparable scale</h3>
        <p>
          Once impacts are named, they still have to be ranked. Not every impact type
          matters equally, and severity runs from a change you can barely detect to the
          loss of a species from a site. I combine expert judgement with published
          evidence to put both of those on defined scales.
        </p>

    <div class="strand">
      <p class="strand__figure">3<span>Where it lands</span></p>
      <div>
        <h3>Invasions in protected areas</h3>
        <p>
          Protected areas are the backbone of conservation policy, and they are not
          closed to invasion. In a global synthesis of 300 reported effects from 44
          invasive species, impacts were negative across population, community and
          ecosystem scales, on every continent with enough data to test. Which raises
          the obvious follow-up: does protection make any measurable difference?
        </p>
        <p>
          Related: <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2024.174823" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Biological invasions negatively impact global protected areas</a>.
        </p>
      </div>
